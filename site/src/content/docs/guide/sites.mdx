pagecue works on most pages as they are. This page covers the cases that need a word.

## Site access

pagecue asks for no access to your sites when you install it. It reaches a page only when you click its button there, and that access lasts until the tab reloads or goes to another page.

To skip the click on a site you present often, allow pagecue there for good. When a page reloads, the prompter says **The page reloaded** and offers **Always reconnect on** *site*. Chrome asks you to confirm. From then on, pagecue reconnects by itself on that site.

To take that access back, use Chrome: right-click the pagecue icon, or open its **Details** in `chrome://extensions`, and change its site access.

## Pages that scroll inside a panel

Dashboards and apps often scroll an inner panel instead of the whole page. pagecue finds the panel that holds the cues and follows that one.

## Single-page apps

When an app changes its address without reloading (a new tab in a dashboard, a route in a web app), pagecue loads the cues for the new address. If the address changes as you scroll the same page, pagecue keeps the same cues.

Addresses are compared without tracking tags (`utm_…`, `fbclid`, `gclid` and similar), so a link from an email finds the same cues.

## Pages in a frame

Some pages show their content inside a frame that fills the window: Claude artifacts, Streamlit apps, embedded reports. pagecue follows the frame.

If the frame comes from another site, pagecue needs your permission to read it. The prompter says **This page shows its content in a frame** and offers **Allow on** *site*, for example `claudeusercontent.com` for Claude artifacts. Chrome asks you once.

A Claude artifact works both on its own page and in the panel beside the chat. Cues written inside the artifact load as soon as it has drawn itself. See [claude.ai and the Claude app](/guide/claude/apps/).

## Local files

Pages you open from your computer (`file://…`) work once you allow it: open `chrome://extensions`, click **Details** under pagecue, and turn on **Allow access to file URLs**.

<Aside type="note">
Chrome doesn't let extensions read a cue file linked from a local page. Put the cues inside the page, or add them with **⋯ → Add cues from Claude**. Claude does this for you.
</Aside>

## Where pagecue can't run

Chrome keeps extensions off its own pages (`chrome://…`), the Chrome Web Store and its PDF viewer. The prompter says **pagecue can't run here**.
